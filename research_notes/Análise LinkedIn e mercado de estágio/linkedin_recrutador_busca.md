# How recruiters find and filter internship/entry-level candidates on LinkedIn, and which profile elements measurably increase visibility (2025–2026)

All findings below come from web-search result snippets. Where a snippet summarizes an official LinkedIn Help/Talent page, the official URL is cited, but I could not open it to verify the exact wording. Treat the numbers as "reported by X". Evidence quality is flagged inline as **[OFFICIAL]** (LinkedIn page or LinkedIn data quoted by a major outlet), **[3RD-PARTY]** (vendor or blog, secondary), **[OLD]** (pre-2021), and **[UNVERIFIED]** (no primary source traceable).

## Q1. Which profile fields does LinkedIn Recruiter search and filter on, and how do keyword/Boolean searches match headline, About and experience text?

### Takeaway
Recruiter combines a free-text **Keywords** box (Boolean) with 40+ structured filters: job title (current/past), location, company, **skills**, school, **graduation year**, degree, industry, **spoken languages**, years of experience, **Open to Work** and job-seeker preferences (employment type, workplace type). Keywords match text across the whole profile: headline, About/Summary, experience titles and descriptions, and Skills. The **title** filter matches structured position titles. Since 2024, AI-assisted (semantic) search turns natural-language requests into these filters. It matches meaning and not only exact words, but it still relies on the text and skills stored on the profile. Spotlights then boost candidates who are likely to respond: Open to Work, Active Talent, company connections and people interested in the company.

### Cited Findings
- **Keyword matching locations [OFFICIAL, via snippet]:** Recruiter highlights search keywords "on the candidate's profile card, in the Summary section, in the Experience section (in the header, description, or location), and in the Skills section." Practitioners describe the Keywords field as "the broadest one... it scans the entire profile: headline, summary, experience, and skills." — [LinkedIn Recruiter Help: Use Boolean to filter search results](https://www.linkedin.com/help/recruiter/answer/a415295); [Leonar – LinkedIn Boolean Search](https://www.leonar.app/blog/linkedin-recruiter-boolean-search/)
- **Title vs keywords [3RD-PARTY]:** you cannot restrict the Recruiter keyword box to titles only. To find people by current position, recruiters use the separate **Job Title** filter with its "Current / Past / Current or Past / Past not current" options. Otherwise results can include people who only mention the term somewhere in their text. — [Leonar – Recruiter Boolean](https://www.leonar.app/blog/linkedin-recruiter-boolean-search/); [HeroHunt – Ultimate LinkedIn Recruiter 2025 Guide](https://www.herohunt.ai/blog/the-ultimate-linkedin-recruiter-2025-guide/)
- **Filter set and "Can have / Must have / Doesn't have" logic [3RD-PARTY, consistent with Help-page title]:** dropdown filters exist for job titles, location, companies, **skills and assessments**, schools, industries and **spoken languages**, each set to Can have / Must have / Doesn't have. The Job Title filter also has an **"Open to work"** option. — [HeroHunt 2025 guide](https://www.herohunt.ai/blog/the-ultimate-linkedin-recruiter-2025-guide/); [LinkedIn Recruiter Help: Filter search results in Recruiter and Recruiter Lite](https://www.linkedin.com/help/recruiter/answer/a414281)
- **Tier differences [3RD-PARTY]:** the **Open to Work filter** is available only in full **Recruiter (Corporate)**, reported at US$835+/month, and **not in Recruiter Lite**. The same source lists Recruiter-only filters for skills and assessments, spoken-language proficiency, **workplace preferences (remote/hybrid/on-site)**, salary expectations, **employment type**, **education level**, "recently joined LinkedIn", seniority, years in current position and years in current company. — [Leonar – LinkedIn Recruiter Search Filters (2026)](https://www.leonar.app/blog/linkedin-recruiter-search-filters/) (via search summary)
- **Years of experience [3RD-PARTY]:** "LinkedIn calculates the number of years" from the positions on the profile, and the recruiter sets a range. — [Skylead – Recruiter filters](https://skylead.io/blog/find-employees-using-linkedin-recruiter-search-filters/)
- **Graduation Year filter for students [OFFICIAL community tip]:** LinkedIn's talent community tells recruiters, "Searching for recent grads? Use the Graduation Year filter in Recruiter." A third-party source warns that the filter reads **every** education entry (including bootcamps and certificates), so it can mislead. — [LinkedIn Talent Community product tip](https://tcommunity.linkedin.com/product-tips-190/searching-for-recent-grads-use-the-graduation-year-filter-in-recruiter-660); [Leonar (via snippet)](https://www.leonar.app/blog/linkedin-recruiter-search-filters/)
- **AI-assisted search [3RD-PARTY citing LinkedIn releases]:** AI-Assisted Search became available to **all English-language Recruiter Corporate customers in May 2024**, with "deeper contextual matching" in the Wave 1 2025 release. Recruiters type a plain-language brief and the system converts it into filters. Semantic matching means a "data analyst" request can surface people described as "business intelligence" or "data visualisation". — [HeroHunt – Recruiter 2025 AI features](https://www.herohunt.ai/blog/linkedin-recruiter-new-ai-features/); [LinkedIn – AI-Assisted Search and Projects (official product page)](https://business.linkedin.com/talent-solutions/ai-assisted-search-and-projects); [LinkedIn Recruiter Help – Search with AI-Assisted Search](https://www.linkedin.com/help/recruiter/answer/a6509735)
- **Hiring Assistant [3RD-PARTY citing LinkedIn]:** LinkedIn's recruiter AI agent was announced in October 2024 and went **generally available in English at the end of September 2025**. It is reported to be available in English, German and French, with more languages in 2026. "AI-Assisted Messages" are reported as "coming soon" in Portuguese, among other languages. Pilot users reportedly reviewed 62% fewer profiles and saved 4+ hours per role. — [Pin.com – LinkedIn Recruiter AI Features 2026](https://www.pin.com/blog/linkedin-recruiter-new-features/); [LinkedIn – Hiring Assistant (official product page)](https://business.linkedin.com/hire/hiring-assistant)
- **Spotlights [OFFICIAL, via snippet]:** Spotlights "help prioritize candidates who are more likely to engage with you and your organization, based on activity, interests, and relationship insights." There are four buckets:
  - **Open to Work**
  - **Active Talent**: recently updated profile, shared a resume with recruiters, responds to InMails, or works at a company with recent layoffs
  - **Company Connections**: 1st-degree connections to the company's employees
  - **Interested in Your Company**: followed the company page or engaged with its content

  — [LinkedIn Recruiter Help – Spotlights in Recruiter and Jobs](https://www.linkedin.com/help/recruiter/answer/a414283); [My Career GPS – Spotlight filters](https://www.mycareergps.com/job-blog/linkedin-spotlight-filters)
- **Skills weight [3RD-PARTY opinion]:** "skills match [is] probably... the most heavily weighted factor" in ranking. This is practitioner speculation; LinkedIn does not publish ranking weights. — [HeroHunt](https://www.herohunt.ai/blog/linkedin-recruiter-new-ai-features/)
- **Skills-based search expands pools [OFFICIAL, Economic Graph, March 2025]:** switching hiring searches from past job titles to explicit skills increases eligible candidates **about 6x** on average (**8.2x** for AI roles, about **3.5x** for green roles). The switch could raise women's share of AI talent pools by up to 24%. — [LinkedIn Economic Graph – Skills-Based Hiring (March 2025, PDF)](https://economicgraph.linkedin.com/content/dam/me/economicgraph/en-us/PDF/skills-based-hiring-march-2025.pdf)

### Inferences
- For a student with no position titled "Estagiário de Engenharia Elétrica" or "Analista de BI", the **Skills section** (Python, Power BI, Excel, AutoCAD, SQL, etc.) and the **headline and About text** are the main hooks. Title-based searches will not find him. Skills-based and AI semantic search are exactly the mechanisms that can.
- Education entries with an **expected graduation year** feed the Graduation Year filter that recruiters use for interns and recent graduates. Both courses should list start and expected end years. The incomplete UNIP course could be marked so it doesn't distort the graduation-year signal. This is my inference; I found no official guidance on it.
- "Location" is a core filter, so the profile location should be set to the metro area recruiters actually search.

### Gaps
- I could not open the Recruiter Help pages, so the complete official filter list (including exact filter names such as "Job seeker preferences" and "Profile language") is unverified.
- I found no official statement on whether Recruiter's keyword search indexes the **Featured** or **Projects** sections, or on how the headline is weighted relative to other fields.
- I found no source on how "Santana de Parnaíba" maps to recruiter location filters (whether it is included in "São Paulo e Região" / "Greater São Paulo").
- It is unconfirmed whether AI-Assisted Search in Recruiter works in **Portuguese** as of 2026. Sources only confirm English at launch.

## Q2. What official LinkedIn statistics exist on profile completeness and Open to Work, and which are old or unverifiable?

### Takeaway
The most-repeated "profile completeness" multipliers (photo 21x/9x/36x, 5+ skills 17x/33x, All-Star 40x) are **LinkedIn-originated but old (about 2016–2017)**. They were never re-published with current methodology and circulate through aggregators. The **newest official numbers** concern Open to Work:
- Open to Work "doubles" the chance of a recruiter message.
- The public frame adds about 40% more recruiter InMails (first published in 2020 and repeated by LinkedIn to CNBC in January 2025).
- 220M+ members have Open to Work turned on (January 2025).

## Q3. How do LinkedIn Skills work now (skill-based matching, assessments, "top skills", skills linked to experiences), and how many are allowed or recommended?

### Takeaway
A profile can hold **up to 100 skills**. Up to **5 "Top skills"** can be pinned in the About section. Skills can be **attached to specific experiences, education, projects and certifications**, and LinkedIn suggests about 5 per role. **Skill Assessments and their badges are gone:** tests stopped in late 2023 and badges were removed in 2024. Skills proof now comes from context: skills linked to jobs, projects and courses.

### Cited Findings
- **Maximum skills [OFFICIAL, via snippet]:** LinkedIn raised the cap from 50 to **100 skills**. The Help page says you can add up to 100 and reorder them. — [LinkedIn Help – Add and remove skills on your profile](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a549047); [Career Horizons – 100 skills](https://www.career-horizons.com/linkedin-tip-100-skills-lots-of-new-changes/)
- **Top skills (About section) [3RD-PARTY; feature introduced about 2023]:** users can choose **up to five top skills** shown with the About section. LinkedIn preloads five based on the profile, and the user can change them. Forbes covered this in June 2023 as a "new LinkedIn feature". — [Forbes, 13 June 2023 (Robin Ryan)](https://www.forbes.com/sites/robinryan/2023/06/13/new-linkedin-feature-youll-want-to-use/); [LinkedIn Makeover, May 2023](https://www.linkedin-makeover.com/2023/05/17/top-skills-linkedin-profile-about-section/)
- **Unverified claim:** "LinkedIn's search algorithm prioritizes these top 5 skills... pulls from these top 5 first." No official source found; treat as practitioner belief. — [search summary of leaders.social / careerology](https://careerology.org/2025/09/04/linkedin-skills-section/)
- **Skills tied to experience and projects [3RD-PARTY]:** you can add about the top 5 skills used in each role when editing an Experience entry. Projects must be linked to an Experience or Education entry and have their own Skills field. — [Job-Hunt.org – LinkedIn Projects section](https://www.job-hunt.org/linkedin-projects/); [JobStars – Adding projects](https://jobstars.com/adding-projects-on-linkedin/); [LinkedIn Talent Blog – How to add skills to your profile](https://www.linkedin.com/business/talent/blog/talent-acquisition/skills-on-linkedin-profile)
- **Skill Assessments discontinued [OFFICIAL]:** LinkedIn Help pages are titled "**Skill Assessments – no longer available**" on the member, Recruiter and Talent Hub help centers. Third-party timelines say the wind-down was announced in late 2023, tests stopped then, and badges and assessment data were removed in 2024. — [LinkedIn Help a1690529](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a1690529); [LinkedIn Recruiter Help a1690529](https://www.linkedin.com/help/recruiter/answer/a1690529); [Medium – Skill assessments no longer available](https://medium.com/@Neelesh-Janga/linkedin-skill-assessments-no-longer-available-0598ccef1464); [Pigment – what proves a skill now](https://pigment.is/blogs/blogs/linkedin-skills-assessment); [Eric Cameron LinkedIn post, about December 2023](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/ericcameron_linkedin-is-doing-away-with-their-skill-assessments-activity-7141293224542826496-aaeV)
- **Recruiter still shows a "Skills and assessments" filter** (legacy name) — [HeroHunt](https://www.herohunt.ai/blog/the-ultimate-linkedin-recruiter-2025-guide/)
- **Skills-first push [OFFICIAL]:** the LinkedIn Talent Blog runs a "skills-first" series. It defines companies with the most skills-based searches as those with 25%+ of recruiters in the top quartile of skills-filter usage. See also the Economic Graph 6x pool expansion in Q1. — [LinkedIn Talent Blog – Business case for skills-first hiring](https://www.linkedin.com/business/talent/blog/talent-acquisition/business-case-for-skills-first-hiring); [LinkedIn Economic Graph – Skills-First report](https://economicgraph.linkedin.com/research/skills-first-report)
- **Old stat [OLD ~2016]:** 5+ skills gives up to 33x more messages and 17x more views (see Q2).

### Inferences
- Recommendation for the student:
  - Fill the Skills section well beyond 5, with roughly 20–50 relevant hard skills. Suggested examples: Python, Power BI, Microsoft Excel, DAX, SQL (if true), AutoCAD, React, Análise de Dados / Data Analysis, Business Intelligence, Circuitos Elétricos, NR-10 (if held), Atendimento ao Cliente, Gestão de Estoque, Liderança de Equipe.
  - Pin 5 Top skills that match the target roles, for example Python, Power BI, Excel, AutoCAD, Análise de Dados.
  - Attach skills to each Experience, Education and Project entry.

  The 100-skill cap, the 5 top-skills slots and the per-entry linking are all current features. The "33x" figure supports the minimum of 5 but is old.
- With assessments gone, recruiters verify skills through **context**: projects, courses and certificates. That argues for adding a Projects entry (for example a Power BI dashboard or Python analysis) linked to the Estácio or UNIP education entry.

### Gaps
- I found no official LinkedIn statement on whether "Top skills" or skills attached to experiences carry more weight in Recruiter ranking than skills merely listed.
- I found no official post-2023 statistic on how the number of skills affects visibility.

## Q4. What do recruiters and LinkedIn say about the public green "Open to Work" frame vs "Recruiters only" visibility, and is there evidence of stigma, especially for students and interns?

### Takeaway
LinkedIn's data points one way: Open to Work (either mode) doubles recruiter messages, and the public frame adds about 40% more recruiter InMails. The **"Recruiters only"** mode is visible only to paid **LinkedIn Recruiter** users. LinkedIn tries to hide it from recruiters at your current employer but "can't guarantee complete privacy". Evidence of stigma is weak, anecdotal, and concentrated in executive or agency search. For students seeking internships, where being available is expected, I found no evidence of a penalty.

### Cited Findings
- **Two visibility modes [OFFICIAL, via snippet]:** "Recruiters only" shows the signal to LinkedIn Recruiter users, with no photo frame. LinkedIn "takes steps to prevent LinkedIn Recruiter users who work at your current company from seeing your shared career interests, but it **can't guarantee complete privacy**." "All LinkedIn members" adds the green #OpenToWork frame. — [LinkedIn Help – Let recruiters know you're Open to Work](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a507508/let-recruiters-know-you-re-open-to-work?lang=en); [PrepNPlaced – who can see it](https://www.prepnplaced.com/blog/linkedin-open-to-work-who-can-see)
- **Preferences you set [3RD-PARTY, consistent across guides]:**
  - up to **5 job titles**
  - **employment types**: full-time, part-time, contract, **internship**, temporary
  - **workplace**: on-site, hybrid, remote
  - up to **5 locations**
  - **start date**: "Immediately, I am actively applying" or "Flexible, I am casually looking"

  — [Teal – Open to Work](https://www.tealhq.com/post/linkedin-open-to-work); [Resume.co](https://resume.co/blog/how-to-enable-open-to-work-on-linkedin); [LinkedIn Help – Job recommendations based on your preferences](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a512279)
- **Effect sizes [OFFICIAL]:**
  - Open to Work "doubles" the chance of a recruiter message, and the public badge raises that "by 40%" (LinkedIn to CNBC, January 2025).
  - 220M+ members have it on, up 35% year on year.

  — [CNBC, 23 January 2025](https://www.cnbc.com/2025/01/23/recruiters-weigh-in-if-linkedins-open-to-work-feature-helps-or-hurts.html)
- **Scale of the signal in Recruiter [3RD-PARTY]:** "Open to Work" is described as the strongest Spotlight, shown in a priority view — [My Career GPS](https://www.mycareergps.com/job-blog/linkedin-spotlight-filters); [Careerflow](https://www.careerflow.ai/blog/how-recruiters-use-linkedin)
- **Stigma, practitioner views:**
  - Stigma "is concentrated in... agency, boutique, and executive search." To most recruiters the badge "does not make you look desperate" — [Hiration (2026)](https://www.hiration.com/blog/does-open-to-work-hurt/)
  - Recruitment consultant Tiffany Dyba, quoted by CNBC: "I think there are far more desperate practices on LinkedIn" — [CNBC 2025](https://www.cnbc.com/2025/01/23/recruiters-weigh-in-if-linkedins-open-to-work-feature-helps-or-hurts.html)
  - Industry debate piece — [ERE – The Debate Over "Open to Work"](https://www.ere.net/articles/the-debate-over-open-to-work)
- **Survey evidence of negative perception [UNVERIFIED]:** the "2024 Resume Builder survey of 1,000 hiring managers" reportedly found **22%** less likely to consider a candidate with the public frame, **18%** positive and **60%** no effect. It surfaced only in an aggregator summary, and I could not find the original. — search summary (source among [Hiration](https://www.hiration.com/blog/does-open-to-work-hurt/) / [Resume Optimizer Pro](https://resumeoptimizerpro.com/blog/linkedin-open-to-work))
- **Limitation of "Recruiters only" [3RD-PARTY]:** only paid LinkedIn Recruiter seats see it, and the Open to Work filter is reportedly absent from Recruiter Lite. Small companies and hiring managers using free LinkedIn or Lite may not see the private signal. — [PrepNPlaced](https://www.prepnplaced.com/blog/linkedin-open-to-work-who-can-see); [Leonar](https://www.leonar.app/blog/linkedin-recruiter-search-filters/)

### Inferences
- For an unemployed or student candidate seeking **estágio**, the main reason for "Recruiters only" (hiding the search from a current employer) mostly doesn't apply. The public frame reaches the free-LinkedIn hiring managers and small firms that don't pay for Recruiter, and LinkedIn's data shows +40% InMails. So the evidence favors **public** mode, with preferences set to **Estágio / Internship** plus relevant titles (for example "Estagiário de Engenharia Elétrica", "Estagiário de Dados", "Estagiário de BI"), hybrid/on-site, and São Paulo-area locations.
- Stigma evidence relates to senior/executive candidates. I found no data on students specifically. This is an extrapolation.

### Gaps
- There are no Brazil-specific or intern-specific data on Open to Work effects.
- LinkedIn's claimed effect sizes are observational (members who opt in may differ), and LinkedIn has not published methodology.

## Q5. Which current (2025–2026) LinkedIn features matter for students (Featured, Projects, custom URL, Creator mode changes, AI job matching, application filters, Portuguese vs English profiles)?

### Takeaway
- **Creator mode is gone.** The toggle was removed in March 2024 and profile hashtags in February 2024; its tools and the "Follow" button now apply by default.
- **Featured and Projects** are the main places to show proof now that assessments are gone.
- The **headline allows 220 characters**.
- **Secondary-language profiles** show viewers the version in their interface language. Keyword search matches stored text, so bilingual keywords matter.
- **AI-powered job search** launched in 2025 (English, Premium first) and expanded globally in **Portuguese** and four other languages around January 2026. It is reported at 1.3M daily users.
- AI "job match" tells applicants how well they fit a job.

### Cited Findings

## Q6. How does LinkedIn job search matching for internships work in Brazil (the "Estágio" experience-level filter, Easy Apply)?

### Takeaway
On LinkedIn job search in pt-BR, "Estágio" appears in two places:
- as an **experience-level** filter: Estágio, Assistente, Júnior, Pleno-sênior, Diretor, Executivo (English: Internship, Entry level, Associate, Mid-Senior, Director, Executive)
- as a **job type**: tempo integral, meio período, contrato, temporário, **estágio**

The experience level comes from the **seniority level the employer sets** when posting, auto-filled from the job title. "Candidatura simplificada" (Easy Apply) sends the LinkedIn profile, plus a CV and screening questions, directly. Brazil is LinkedIn's 3rd-largest market with **100M members (June 2026)**.

### Inferences
